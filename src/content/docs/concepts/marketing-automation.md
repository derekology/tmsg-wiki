---
title: Marketing Automation
description: The systems and rules that coordinate repeatable marketing tasks and customer communications.
---

Marketing automation uses software and defined workflows to coordinate repeatable marketing processes, such as audience selection, triggered messages, campaign steps, and lead follow-up. It is not simply “sending more email.” A useful automation responds to a meaningful customer action or business rule and has a clear purpose, owner, and way to stop or recover from errors.

## Anatomy of a workflow

A workflow usually has:

1. **An entry condition**, such as a permitted signup or product event.
2. **Eligibility rules**, including consent, suppression, and audience criteria.
3. **Actions**, such as sending a message, updating a record, or routing a task.
4. **Timing and branching**, based on relevant changes or responses.
5. **Exit and failure handling**, so the person does not receive inappropriate repeats and staff can detect problems.

For instance, a webinar registration may trigger a confirmation, a reminder, and a follow-up resource. A sales handoff should happen only when the team has agreed that the signal is relevant and the recipient has a clear owner.

## Risks and operating practices

Automation can amplify stale data, ambiguous definitions, and poor timing. Keep a workflow inventory, review privacy and consent requirements, test edge cases, monitor send and synchronization errors, and retire flows that no longer serve their purpose. Do not interpret an automated score or activity signal as a complete picture of a person's intent.

Platforms such as [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/), [Adobe Marketo Engage](/tools/marketo-engage/), [Klaviyo](/tools/klaviyo/), and [Mailchimp](/tools/mailchimp/) provide different combinations of these capabilities. Compare workflows, data, governance, and operations—not just the number of automation features.
