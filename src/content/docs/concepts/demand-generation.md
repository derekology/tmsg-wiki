---
title: Demand Generation and Demand Capture
description: How marketers create future interest and serve buyers already looking for a solution.
---

Demand generation and demand capture describe complementary jobs in a marketing system. **Demand generation** builds awareness, understanding, and preference among people who may not be actively shopping yet. **Demand capture** helps people who are already researching a solution find and choose an offer. Organizations use the terms differently, so agree on the operational meaning before using them to set targets or assign budgets.

## A connected system

Demand generation can include useful education, category narratives, communities, events, and brand activity. Demand capture often includes search visibility, comparison information, review presence, conversion paths, and timely sales follow-up. A prospect may move between these activities more than once; the two are not mutually exclusive campaign types.

The connection matters: if awareness work creates interest but there is no clear way to learn more or buy, demand may go elsewhere. If a company focuses only on people already searching, it may miss future buyers and become dependent on current intent. Plan both around the customer problem, not only around channel labels.

## Measurement

Capture programs can often report direct responses and conversions, though attribution remains imperfect. Demand generation may influence familiarity and later consideration without producing an immediate form fill. Use a mix of measures: reach and engagement for learning, qualified opportunities and pipeline for business outcomes, and experiments or holdouts when estimating incremental impact.

Do not call every lead a result of demand creation, or every search click proof of demand capture. Record assumptions and avoid comparing programs with incompatible time horizons.
