title: Marketing Technology

Marketing technology (martech) is the software and connected infrastructure used to plan, create, deliver, personalize, coordinate, and measure marketing. A martech stack is the set of these tools and their data flows in a particular organization. The stack is only one part of a [marketing system](/concepts/marketing-systems/), which also includes people, processes, data definitions, and governance.

## Common categories

An organization may use technology for:

- Customer and account records, such as CRM systems.
- Campaign planning, marketing automation, and customer engagement.
- Content management, digital experiences, and personalization.
- Data collection, analytics, attribution, and experimentation.
- Customer data management, integration, and audience activation.
- Consent, preference, identity, and data-quality operations.
- Email creation, delivery, testing, and deliverability monitoring.

Category boundaries overlap, and product capabilities vary. A label such as “marketing platform” does not establish whether a product fits a particular workflow, data model, scale, or compliance need.

## Evaluate by the work to be done

Begin with a business or customer problem and a written set of requirements. Map current processes and information flows before comparing products. Then assess:

1. **Functional fit:** Does the tool support the required use cases, channels, and customer journey?
2. **Data fit:** Can it work with agreed identifiers, definitions, data quality, consent, and retention requirements?
3. **Integration fit:** What systems exchange which data, in what direction, with what latency and failure handling?
4. **Operational fit:** Who configures, supports, secures, trains, and monitors it? How much ongoing work does it add?
5. **Control and portability:** Can teams manage access, audit important actions, export data, and leave or change the solution?
6. **Economics and risk:** Consider total cost, implementation effort, contract terms, vendor dependency, and the consequences of failure.

Prototype the critical workflow with representative data and test edge cases before committing to a broad rollout. Assign a product owner and integration owners; document what is authoritative in each system to avoid conflicting records.

## Avoid stack-first decisions

More integrations or overlapping platforms can increase cost and failure modes without improving customer outcomes. Consolidate only after understanding which teams and workflows depend on each tool. Conversely, a single suite may not satisfy every specialized need. The right architecture balances usability, reliability, governance, and the organization's ability to operate it.
