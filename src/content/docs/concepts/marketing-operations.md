title: Marketing Operations
description: The people, processes, data, and systems that make marketing work repeatably and measurably.

Marketing operations (often shortened to marketing ops or MOPs) is the operating capability that helps a marketing organization plan, execute, measure, and improve its work. It commonly spans campaign processes, technology administration, data quality, measurement, governance, and enablement. Its remit varies by organization; it should be defined by the outcomes and responsibilities the team owns, rather than by a universal job description.

## Core responsibilities

- **Planning and workflow:** Translate priorities into capacity-aware plans, briefs, approvals, calendars, and repeatable campaign processes.
- **Technology and integration:** Administer platforms, manage access and configuration, document integrations, and coordinate change and incident handling.
- **Data and governance:** Establish common definitions, quality checks, consent and preference handling, retention rules, and accountable data ownership.
- **Measurement:** Define reporting requirements and campaign conventions; validate data flows and explain assumptions behind performance reporting.
- **Enablement:** Help teams use processes and tools consistently through documentation, training, and support.

These responsibilities connect strategy to execution. For example, a lifecycle campaign is not ready merely because its email is designed: its audience, consent basis, entry and exit logic, CRM or analytics effects, monitoring, and owner need to be clear.

## Establish an operating model

Begin with an inventory of recurring work, systems, data, and handoffs. For each important process, document the accountable owner, inputs, approvals, expected outputs, and exception path. Define shared terms with sales, product, finance, privacy, and IT where responsibilities overlap. Establish a small set of operational measures—such as launch reliability, data completeness, workflow error rates, and time to execute—alongside campaign and business outcomes.

Marketing ops should not become a ticket desk for every request or a technology team isolated from strategy. Prioritize controls and improvements according to risk, customer impact, and organizational goals. Keep policies understandable, automate checks where practical, and review processes when the underlying customer journey or technology changes.

Marketing operations is one component of a broader [marketing system](/concepts/marketing-systems/), and often works closely with [revenue operations](/concepts/revenue-operations/) and [marketing automation](/concepts/marketing-automation/).
