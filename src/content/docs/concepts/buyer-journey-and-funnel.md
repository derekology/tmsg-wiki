---
title: Buyer Journey and Marketing Funnel
description: Use journey maps and funnels together without mistaking a model for how every buyer behaves.
---

The **buyer journey** describes the activities, questions, and decisions people move through while addressing a need and evaluating a purchase. The **marketing funnel** is a simplified model for organizing how a business attracts, engages, and converts potential customers. Common funnel labels include top, middle, and bottom of funnel (TOFU, MOFU, and BOFU).

The two models are related but not identical. A journey focuses on the buyer's experience; a funnel often focuses on the organization's process and measurable transitions. Neither should be treated as a universal sequence. Buyers may research independently, revisit earlier questions, involve colleagues, or engage with a company only late in the process.

## Turn the model into useful work

Start with customer evidence: interviews, support questions, search behavior, sales notes, and product analytics. For each meaningful situation, record the buyer's question, useful information, likely next step, and how the business can help. Then decide where funnel stages are useful for planning and reporting.

Do not assume a content asset maps to exactly one stage or that every person moves linearly. A comparison guide may help an early researcher and a buyer near a decision. Build routes between useful material rather than forcing every visitor through the same sequence.

## Measure transitions carefully

Define stage entry and exit rules before calculating conversion rates. A funnel can show where observed records drop out, but not necessarily why. Differences may reflect data capture, eligibility rules, timing, or channel mix. Pair funnel analysis with qualitative evidence and cohort analysis.
