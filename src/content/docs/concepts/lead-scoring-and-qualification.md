---
title: Lead Scoring and Qualification
description: Prioritize follow-up with transparent criteria and clear MQL-to-SQL agreements.
---

**Lead scoring** assigns values or categories to observed fit and engagement signals to help prioritize follow-up. **Lead qualification** is the broader process of deciding whether an individual, account, or opportunity is appropriate for a particular next step. An **MQL** (marketing qualified lead) and **SQL** (sales qualified lead) are common lifecycle labels, but organizations define them differently.

## Build a useful model

Start by agreeing on what the score should help someone decide. Separate fit signals (such as a relevant role or organization profile) from engagement signals (such as a meaningful response to a campaign). Choose criteria based on evidence from successful and unsuccessful outcomes, and document how each signal affects priority.

Set explicit MQL and SQL rules with sales and marketing. Include who reviews a handoff, how quickly it should happen, what counts as acceptance or rejection, and how rejected records are followed up. Audit the result by segment: a model can appear effective overall while disadvantaging a customer group or flooding sales with low-value contacts.

## Limits and safeguards

A score is a prioritization aid, not a probability of purchase unless it has been designed and validated as a predictive model. Opens, clicks, and repeated visits can be noisy or automated. Scores become stale as customer behavior and the market change; monitor false positives, false negatives, and outcomes over time.

Do not use sensitive or inappropriate personal characteristics. Make the criteria explainable to the teams acting on them, and provide a way to override or correct bad data.
