title: Go-to-Market Strategy
description: How a go-to-market strategy connects a target market, customer value, channels, and execution.

A go-to-market (GTM) strategy is the coordinated plan for bringing an offering to a defined market and helping intended customers discover, evaluate, buy, and use it. It connects business goals to customer needs, positioning, routes to market, and the work teams must do before and after launch. A launch campaign is one possible part of a GTM strategy, not the whole strategy.

## Build the strategy around a customer and an outcome

A useful GTM plan makes explicit:

- **Market and audience:** Which segment, use case, or customer problem is in scope? What evidence supports the choice?
- **Value and positioning:** What outcome does the offering enable, what alternatives does it replace, and why should the audience believe the difference?
- **Offer and commercial model:** What is included, how is it packaged, and how do pricing, procurement, and sales motion fit the audience?
- **Routes to market:** Which direct, partner, product-led, or other channels can reach and serve the audience?
- **Customer journey and operations:** How will marketing, sales, product, support, and operations coordinate discovery, qualification, onboarding, and feedback?
- **Measures and assumptions:** Which leading indicators and business outcomes define success, and what would cause the team to revise the plan?

The choices should reinforce one another. A complex enterprise sale, for example, needs a different qualification, proof, and implementation path than a self-serve product. The right design depends on the buying context, the team's capacity, and the economics of serving the segment.

## Turn the plan into coordinated work

Translate the strategy into owners, dependencies, dates, and decision points. Align the audience definition and lifecycle terms across teams; prepare the product, sales materials, campaigns, data, and service processes; and test the handoffs before volume increases. After launch, compare actual customer response and operational results with the assumptions in the plan. Feed what is learned back into positioning, targeting, packaging, and channel choices.

This work depends on a sound [marketing system](/concepts/marketing-systems/) and clear [marketing operations](/concepts/marketing-operations/). A GTM plan may use [marketing technology](/concepts/marketing-technology/) to execute and measure work, but purchasing a platform does not resolve unclear positioning or ownership.
