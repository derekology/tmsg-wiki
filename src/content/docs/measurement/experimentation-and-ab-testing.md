---
title: Experimentation and A/B Testing
description: Design controlled tests that produce interpretable marketing and product decisions.
---

An A/B test is a controlled experiment that compares two variants by randomly assigning eligible participants and measuring a preselected outcome. Marketers use it to test changes to landing pages, messages, offers, or product experiences. It is one form of experimentation; not every marketing question can be answered by an A/B test.

## A disciplined test

1. **State the question and hypothesis.** Explain what change is being tested and why it might affect a specific audience.
2. **Choose one primary outcome.** For example, qualified form submissions rather than clicks alone. Record guardrail metrics such as unsubscribes, errors, or lead quality.
3. **Define the population and assignment.** Randomize consistently and prevent the same person from switching variants where feasible.
4. **Plan the test before launch.** Consider traffic, expected effect, duration, seasonality, and the analysis method. Avoid stopping as soon as results look favorable.
5. **Check implementation and interpret cautiously.** Verify assignment, event collection, and sample balance; report uncertainty and any deviations.

Changing several elements at once can make it difficult to know which caused a result. Repeatedly checking results and stopping at a convenient time can also inflate false positives. Keep a test log and distinguish exploratory findings from confirmatory tests.

## When not to A/B test

If traffic is too low, the change is urgent, or randomization is not ethical or practical, use qualitative research, usability testing, historical analysis, or another design. A test may estimate the effect only for the tested audience and conditions; it does not guarantee the same result elsewhere.
