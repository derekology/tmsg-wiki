---
title: Customer Acquisition Cost and Customer Lifetime Value
description: Use CAC and CLV together while making assumptions explicit.
---

**Customer acquisition cost (CAC)** estimates the cost of acquiring customers over a defined period. A basic calculation divides relevant acquisition costs by the number of new customers acquired during that period. **Customer lifetime value (CLV, LTV, or CLTV)** estimates the value generated by a customer relationship over time. These are useful planning measures, but there is no single calculation that suits every business.

## Define the calculation first

For CAC, specify which costs are included: paid media alone, all marketing, sales costs, tools, agency expenses, or some combination. Decide how to assign shared costs and how long to allow for a campaign or sales cycle to produce customers.

For CLV, decide whether the figure represents revenue, gross profit, contribution margin, or discounted cash flow. A simple recurring-revenue estimate might use average gross profit per customer per period multiplied by expected retained periods. This assumes future behavior resembles the model's historical data; it can be misleading when churn, pricing, margin, or customer mix changes.

## Use them as a pair, not a universal benchmark

Comparing CLV with CAC can help assess acquisition economics, but only when both use compatible cohorts, periods, and cost definitions. A ratio based on optimistic lifetime assumptions can hide a cash-flow problem or poor retention. Track payback period and retention alongside the ratio, and report uncertainty for newer or smaller cohorts.

Segment by acquisition source and customer type where sample sizes support it. Never present a single blended average as proof that every channel or customer is profitable.
