The Controlling the Assault of Non-Solicited Pornography and Marketing Act of 2003 (CAN-SPAM Act) is a United States federal law governing commercial email. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) explains that it covers commercial messages beyond bulk email and does not provide a general exception for business-to-business messages.

**Not legal advice:** This page is a general educational overview, not legal advice or a compliance assessment. Whether a message or organization is covered depends on the facts and current law. Consult qualified legal counsel familiar with U.S. law and the relevant communications for advice.

## What it covers

The Act's requirements include accurate header information, non-deceptive subject lines, identification of advertising where required, a valid physical postal address, and a clear way for recipients to opt out of future commercial messages. It also limits how opt-out requests and email addresses collected for that purpose may be used. The Act does not generally require prior opt-in consent for commercial email, but other laws and rules may apply to the same message.

The FTC notes that the Act applies to business-to-business email as well as consumer email, and that both the promoted company and a sender may have responsibility in some circumstances. For example, an email promoting a product may be a commercial message even when it is sent to existing customers; a mixed-purpose message can require analysis of its primary purpose under the FTC's rules.
