The UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR), together with the Data Protection Act 2018, forms a central part of the United Kingdom's data-protection framework. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 amended the UK GDPR, the 2018 Act, and related privacy legislation; the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) reports that the data-protection provisions came into force in stages through June 2026.

**Not legal advice:** This page is a general educational overview, not legal advice or a compliance assessment. The rules, exemptions, and territorial scope depend on the facts and current law. Consult qualified legal counsel familiar with UK data-protection law and the relevant activity for advice.

## What it covers

The UK GDPR covers the processing of personal data and includes principles such as fairness, transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, security, and accountability. It recognizes several lawful bases for processing and provides rights that include access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, and objection, subject to statutory conditions and exceptions. It also sets responsibilities for controllers and processors and addresses matters such as security, international transfers, and personal-data breaches.

The Data Protection Act 2018 supplements the UK GDPR and contains additional provisions, including rules for law-enforcement and intelligence-services processing. Electronic marketing is also addressed by the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR), which are distinct from the UK GDPR. For example, an email campaign using personal data may involve questions under both data-protection and electronic-communications law; the details determine which provisions apply.
