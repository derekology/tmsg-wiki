The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) is California's consumer privacy law. California voters amended it through the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA); the CPRA is an amendment to the CCPA, not a separate law. The statute applies to businesses that meet its definitions and thresholds, with exceptions and other details in the law and regulations.

**Not legal advice:** This page is a general educational overview, not legal advice or a compliance assessment. Applicability and requirements depend on the facts, current law, and regulations. Consult qualified legal counsel familiar with California privacy law and the relevant activity for advice.

## What it covers

The law's definitions of a covered business, consumer, and personal information determine its reach; the business criteria include specified thresholds and other statutory conditions. Among its consumer rights are the right to know what personal information is collected and how it is used or disclosed, access specific information, request deletion or correction, opt out of certain sales or sharing, and limit certain uses and disclosures of sensitive personal information. The law also addresses notice, retention, service-provider relationships, and nondiscrimination.

The California Privacy Protection Agency and the California Attorney General have roles in administering and enforcing the law. For example, an online advertising arrangement may involve the CCPA's statutory concept of sharing personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising. Whether a particular arrangement meets the law's definitions or triggers a specific duty depends on its details.
