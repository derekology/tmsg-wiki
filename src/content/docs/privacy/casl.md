Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) is a federal law addressing spam and other electronic threats. Its provisions cover commercial electronic messages, certain computer-program installations, and changes to transmission data. The law and its regulations include scope rules, exceptions, and detailed conditions.

**Not legal advice:** This page is a general educational overview, not legal advice or a compliance assessment. CASL's application and its exceptions depend on the facts and current law. Consult qualified legal counsel familiar with Canadian law and the relevant communications for advice.

## What it covers

CASL covers commercial electronic messages (CEMs) sent to electronic addresses, subject to its scope rules and exceptions. For covered CEMs, its framework generally addresses consent, identification of the sender and any person on whose behalf the message is sent, contact information, and an unsubscribe mechanism. Consent can be express or, in defined circumstances, implied; the statute and regulations specify the relevant conditions and exceptions.

The Act also addresses installing computer programs on another person's computer in the course of commercial activity and altering transmission data in electronic messages. For example, an email or text that encourages participation in a commercial activity may fall within the CEM framework. The law's definitions and exceptions determine whether a particular message or installation is covered.
