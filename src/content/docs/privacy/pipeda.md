The Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) is Canada's federal private-sector privacy law. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC) describes it as applying to personal information handled in commercial activities across Canada and to employee information held by federally regulated businesses. Provincial privacy laws that have been deemed substantially similar affect which rules apply in some circumstances.

**Not legal advice:** This page is a general educational overview, not legal advice or a compliance assessment. PIPEDA's relationship with provincial and sector-specific laws depends on the facts. Consult qualified legal counsel familiar with Canadian privacy law and the relevant activity for advice.

## What it covers

PIPEDA's ten fair information principles address accountability; identifying purposes; consent; limiting collection, use, disclosure, and retention; accuracy; safeguards; openness; individual access; and the ability to challenge an organization's compliance. The Act also includes requirements related to meaningful consent, access to personal information, and breach reporting in specified circumstances.

PIPEDA also applies to personal information that crosses provincial or national borders in the course of commercial activity, while substantially similar provincial laws affect the rules for some activity occurring within those provinces. For example, a customer record handled by a business in a commercial transaction may fall within privacy-law protections; the applicable federal, provincial, or sector-specific framework depends on the organization, information, and activity.
