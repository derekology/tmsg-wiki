The Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) is China's framework for processing personal information. It applies to processing within China and, in defined circumstances, to processing outside China relating to individuals in China, such as processing to provide products or services or analyze behavior there.

**Not legal advice:** This page is a general educational overview, not legal advice or a compliance assessment. PIPL's territorial scope and obligations depend on the facts and current law. Consult qualified legal counsel familiar with Chinese law and the relevant activity for advice.

## What it covers

The PIPL sets rules for personal-information processing, including principles of legality, necessity, good faith, transparency, and limiting collection to what is needed for stated purposes. It specifies several grounds for processing, including consent and certain contractual or legal necessities. Separate provisions cover sensitive personal information, automated decision-making, and the provision of personal information across borders.

Individuals have rights relating to their personal information, including access, copying, correction, supplementation, and deletion, as well as the ability to withdraw consent where consent is the basis. The law also sets obligations for personal-information processors and provides for regulatory supervision and legal liability. For example, an overseas service that analyzes the behavior of people in China may fall within the law's stated extra-territorial scope; the example does not determine whether a particular service is covered.
