Brazil's Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados Pessoais (LGPD), or General Personal Data Protection Law, is the country's broad framework for processing personal data. The law covers public- and private-sector processing in the circumstances it specifies, including certain processing connected to people in Brazil. Brazil's National Data Protection Authority (ANPD) publishes the law and related materials.

**Not legal advice:** This page is a general educational overview, not legal advice or a compliance assessment. The LGPD's scope, amendments, and requirements depend on the facts and current law. Consult qualified legal counsel familiar with Brazilian privacy law and the relevant activity for advice.

## What it covers

The LGPD sets principles and legal bases for processing personal data and defines the roles of controllers and operators. It provides rights that include confirmation of processing, access, correction, anonymization, blocking or deletion in specified cases, portability, information about sharing, and revocation of consent where consent is the basis. It also addresses sensitive personal data, security incidents, international transfers, and oversight by the ANPD.

The law includes territorial criteria for processing in Brazil and certain processing outside the country connected to people in Brazil. For example, an online service handling account details related to people in Brazil may be within the scope described in the law. The specific criteria and exceptions determine whether it applies. The ANPD's English-language text is a reference translation; the Portuguese legal text is authoritative.
