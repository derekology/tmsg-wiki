Australia's Privacy Act 1988 is a federal privacy law that regulates the handling of personal information by Australian Government agencies and covered private-sector organizations. The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) notes that the Act covers organizations above a specified annual-turnover threshold as well as some other organizations; coverage and exceptions are set out in the Act.

**Not legal advice:** This page is a general educational overview, not legal advice or a compliance assessment. Coverage and requirements depend on the facts and current law. Consult qualified legal counsel familiar with Australian privacy law and the relevant activity for advice.

## What it covers

The 13 Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) cover open and transparent information handling; anonymity and pseudonymity; collection; notification; use and disclosure; direct marketing; cross-border disclosure; security; and access to and correction of personal information, among other matters. The Privacy Act also contains definitions, permitted situations, exceptions, and a Notifiable Data Breaches scheme.

The Act's coverage includes Australian Government agencies and many private-sector organizations, with statutory exceptions and additional categories of covered organizations. For example, an organization using personal information for direct marketing may be within the subject addressed by APP 7; whether the Act or a particular requirement applies is a separate, fact-specific question.
