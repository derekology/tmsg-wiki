---
title: Marketing Systems Glossary
description: Concise definitions of marketing, data, measurement, and software terms.
---

This glossary explains terms used across marketing work and its supporting systems. Definitions are practical, not legal or vendor-specific.

## A–C

- **A/B test:** A controlled experiment that randomly assigns eligible participants to variants and measures a preselected outcome. See [experimentation and A/B testing](/measurement/experimentation-and-ab-testing/).
- **Account-based marketing (ABM):** A strategy that coordinates marketing and sales around a defined set of target accounts. See [account-based marketing](/concepts/account-based-marketing/).
- **Agent (AI agent):** Software that uses an AI model to interpret a goal and select actions, sometimes calling tools or services. An agent's actual access is determined by its integrations and permissions.
- **API (application programming interface):** A defined way for software systems to request data or actions from one another.
- **Attribution:** A method for assigning credit for a measured outcome to marketing interactions. A model's credit allocation is not, by itself, proof that an interaction caused the outcome. See [marketing attribution](/measurement/marketing-attribution/).
- **Audience:** A group of people or accounts selected by shared attributes, behaviors, or rules for analysis or marketing activity.
- **Automation:** Software execution of a defined task or workflow when specified conditions are met. Automation follows configured logic; it does not guarantee that the logic or inputs are correct.
- **CAC (customer acquisition cost):** The cost attributed to acquiring customers, calculated using an explicit cost scope, time period, and customer count. See [CAC and customer lifetime value](/measurement/cac-and-customer-lifetime-value/).
- **CDP (customer data platform):** Software for assembling and managing customer data and making it usable by other systems. Product architectures vary. See [customer data platforms](/data/customer-data-platform/).
- **CLV (customer lifetime value; also LTV or CLTV):** An estimate of value generated by a customer relationship over time; the result depends on the value measure, period, and assumptions used. See [CAC and customer lifetime value](/measurement/cac-and-customer-lifetime-value/).
- **Consent:** A person's permission for a defined use of their information or for a particular communication, where applicable. Consent requirements and records depend on context and jurisdiction; a preference field is not automatically equivalent to valid consent.
- **Conversion:** A defined action treated as a meaningful outcome, such as a signup, purchase, or qualified opportunity. A conversion is only interpretable when its event and measurement rules are clear.
- **CRM (customer relationship management):** Software and processes for managing customer, prospect, and account relationships, often supporting sales or service as well as marketing.

## D–M

- **Data activation:** Making data or audience definitions available to systems that use them, such as advertising, CRM, or messaging platforms. See [Hightouch](/tools/hightouch/) for a vendor example.
- **Data warehouse:** A system that stores structured organizational data for reporting and analysis; it can also supply modeled data to operational systems.
- **Demand generation and demand capture:** Demand generation builds awareness, understanding, or preference; demand capture helps people already researching a solution find and evaluate an offer. See [demand generation and demand capture](/concepts/demand-generation/).
- **DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail):** An email authentication method that lets a sender sign selected message content with a private key so a receiver can verify it using a public key. See [DKIM](/lifecycle/dkim/).
- **DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance):** A domain policy and reporting mechanism that checks whether SPF or DKIM passes with alignment to the visible From domain. See [DMARC](/lifecycle/dmarc/).
- **Email marketing:** Using email to communicate with an appropriate audience through campaigns such as lifecycle, triggered, or batch sends. See [email marketing](/email/email-marketing/).
- **Email deliverability:** The likelihood that email is accepted and placed where recipients can find it; authentication, sender reputation, recipient expectations, and filtering all matter. See [email deliverability](/lifecycle/email-deliverability/).
- **Event:** A recorded occurrence, such as a page view, signup, or purchase, usually represented with a name, time, and associated properties.
- **First-party data:** Information an organization collects directly through its own interactions or services, subject to applicable notice, consent, and use restrictions. See [first-party and intent data](/data/first-party-and-intent-data/).
- **Go-to-market (GTM) strategy:** A coordinated plan for taking an offering to a defined market, including its audience, value, routes to market, and execution. See [go-to-market strategy](/concepts/go-to-market/).
- **Headless platform:** A platform whose capabilities can be accessed through interfaces beyond its own graphical application. The original interface may remain available. See [headless marketing platforms](/concepts/headless-marketing-platform/).
- **Ideal customer profile (ICP):** A description of the type of customer for whom an offer is a strong fit and likely to deliver value; in B2B, it usually describes an organization or account. See [ideal customer profile](/concepts/ideal-customer-profile/).
- **Identity resolution:** Rules and processes for deciding which records or identifiers refer to the same person or account. Incorrect matching can merge distinct people or fragment one person's records.
- **In-app messaging:** Content displayed within an app or website while someone is using it, often in response to an audience rule or event. See [in-app messaging](/channels/in-app-messaging/).
- **Intent data:** Behavioral signals used to infer possible interest in a topic, product, or purchase; an observed signal is not a confirmed buying decision. See [first-party and intent data](/data/first-party-and-intent-data/).
- **Integration:** A connection that allows systems to exchange data or invoke actions. Integrations differ in direction, timing, supported fields, failure behavior, and access controls.
- **Lead:** A person or organization recorded as a potential customer. The definition and lifecycle vary by business; it should not be assumed to mean a qualified buying opportunity.
- **Lead scoring:** A model or ruleset that assigns values to leads to help prioritize follow-up. Scores depend on data quality and agreed qualification criteria. See [lead scoring and qualification](/concepts/lead-scoring-and-qualification/).
- **Long code:** A standard telephone number used for SMS; in the United States, a 10-digit long code used for application-to-person messaging is called 10DLC. See [SMS messaging](/channels/sms-messaging/).
- **Lifecycle marketing:** Marketing coordinated to a customer's or prospect's stage and interactions over time, including onboarding, engagement, and retention.
- **MCP (Model Context Protocol):** An open protocol for connecting AI applications to external systems, including tools and data sources. MCP describes a connection mechanism; each server determines what it exposes. See [headless marketing platforms](/concepts/headless-marketing-platform/).
- **MQL (marketing qualified lead):** A lifecycle label for a lead that meets an organization's agreed criteria for marketing-qualified follow-up; definitions vary. See [lead scoring and qualification](/concepts/lead-scoring-and-qualification/).
- **Marketing automation:** Software that runs configured marketing workflows, such as triggered communications or audience-based journeys. See [marketing automation](/concepts/marketing-automation/).
- **Marketing operations (MOps):** The people, processes, data practices, and technology operations that enable marketing teams to plan, execute, measure, and improve work. See [marketing operations](/concepts/marketing-operations/).
- **Martech (marketing technology):** Software and infrastructure used to plan, deliver, coordinate, and measure marketing. See [marketing technology](/concepts/marketing-technology/).
- **Model (data model):** A defined representation of data entities, fields, and relationships used for analysis or system behavior. In AI contexts, “model” can instead mean the machine-learning system that processes inputs and produces outputs.

## N–Z

- **NRR (net revenue retention):** A measure of recurring-revenue change for an existing-customer group over a defined period, including expansion and contraction but excluding new-customer revenue. See [retention and net revenue retention](/lifecycle/retention-and-net-revenue-retention/).
- **Personalization:** Adapting content or experiences using information about an audience or individual. It requires suitable data, clear rules, and appropriate use.
- **Product-led growth (PLG):** A go-to-market strategy in which the product experience is a primary driver of acquisition, expansion, or retention. See [product-led growth](/concepts/product-led-growth/).
- **Retention:** Continued customer activity or relationship over a defined period; the cohort, event, and time window must be specified. See [retention and net revenue retention](/lifecycle/retention-and-net-revenue-retention/).
- **Revenue operations (RevOps):** Coordination of processes, data, and systems across revenue-related teams, commonly marketing, sales, and customer success. See [revenue operations](/concepts/revenue-operations/).
- **Segment:** A subset of records that meet a defined set of criteria. The term can refer to an audience definition or, in a product name, a vendor; it does not imply a particular data architecture.
- **Short code:** A short number used for application-to-person messaging in some markets; availability, registration, and capabilities vary by country and carrier. See [SMS messaging](/channels/sms-messaging/).
- **SMS (Short Message Service):** Text messaging delivered through mobile networks. Business messaging may use long codes or short codes, depending on market and use. See [SMS messaging](/channels/sms-messaging/).
- **SQL (sales qualified lead):** A lifecycle label for a lead that meets an organization's agreed criteria for sales follow-up; definitions and handoff rules vary. See [lead scoring and qualification](/concepts/lead-scoring-and-qualification/).
- **SPF (Sender Policy Framework):** An email authentication method that lets a domain publish which hosts may send using its SMTP identity. See [SPF](/lifecycle/spf/).
- **Sync:** A configured transfer or update of data between systems. A sync may be scheduled or event-driven and can fail, lag, or apply destination-specific transformations.
- **UTM parameters:** Campaign tags added to URLs to help analytics systems classify traffic sources and campaigns. Consistent naming is necessary for useful reporting. See [UTM parameters](/measurement/utm-parameters/).
- **Webhook:** An HTTP message one system sends to another when a specified event occurs, often used to trigger near-real-time integrations.
- **Web personalization:** Adapting website content or experiences for visitors or audiences using defined criteria and available data. See [web personalization](/channels/web-personalization/).
- **Workflow:** A sequence of tasks, decisions, or handoffs designed to achieve a defined operational outcome.
